# Policy Guardian — round 1 (2026-11-18)

**Position.** The gate is green; the app is not uploadable. Two things can kill the launch and both are inside the artefact: (1) the privacy policy and licence offer it ships still call themselves placeholders; (2) the LGPL duty starts at the first tester install, not at production. The Play-console work is downstream of these, and one mandatory App-content declaration is missing from every checklist in the repo (foreground-service types).

## 1. What remains, grounded
- **B1–B7 all OPEN** (`docs/legal/release-blockers.md:10-18`). Verified today on Play Help: privacy policy is mandatory in the listing **and** in-app even with no data access, on a public HTTPS page, worldwide, no login, as a non-editable webpage — not a PDF/downloadable file ([9859455](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455)); Data safety is mandatory and "collect" = transmitting data off the device ([10787469](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)); new apps must ship an AAB ([9844679](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844679)); target API 36 since 2026-08-31 ([11926878](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878)) — this build targets 36, versionCode 3 (`app/build.gradle.kts`).
- **12×14 is stated correctly** (`docs/product/store-listing.md:239-265`): only personal accounts created after 2023-11-13 need a closed test with ≥12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days, then a production-access application (Help 14151465). Use Closed testing, not Internal — internal does not count; the plan must branch on the account type, not assume the 14 days.
- **Data-safety answers are structurally right.** "No collection" matches Play's definition (Help 10787469) because nothing leaves the device; the one network call is user-initiated, carries no user data, and the policy names host + IP (`app/src/main/assets/legal/privacy-policy.md:27-37`); keep the repo's own "re-read the form at submission" caveat (`store-listing.md:190,199-202`).
- **Objection (ship-blocking): the artefact ships unfinished legal text.** The in-app policy says "legal name to be completed" and "placeholder address" (`privacy-policy.md:4-5,65`; Arabic `privacy-policy.ar.md:4-5`), and the written offer is a TODO with a placeholder mailbox (`NOTICE.md:48-50`; `assets/legal/source-offer.txt:28`). Play requires one final policy in-app and at the URL; reviewers and testers read the app's own screen. B2 is a content fix + rebuild, not a hosting chore.
- **Gap: foreground-service declaration.** The service is declared `dataSync` (`app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml:49-53`); targetSdk 36 makes Play's App-content FGS declaration mandatory — description, user impact, use-case choice and **a link to a demo video** ([13392821](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)). `dataSync`'s use-case chart includes "Local processing: Import or export / Other", so the current type is defensible, but the choice is explicitly undecided (`docs/product/permissions-rationale.md:64-72`) and no B-item or §9 gate mentions the declaration. Android 15 also caps `dataSync` at 6 h/24 h and calls `onTimeout` ([Android 15 behaviour changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)) — the service must handle it.
- **Objection (ordering):** `release-blockers.md:20-22` says B6/B7 close "before the first public artefact" and that "any distribution of the APK triggers the LGPL duties". A closed test is distribution to 12+ third parties, so the upload to the closed track is the trigger. **I downgrade my own WS4 phrase "public artefact" to "first conveyance".**
- **UX P1s are not Play blockers** (`docs/council/2026-11-18-ui-review/council-review.md:259-264`), but the batch-outcome P1 is a *cohort-continuity* risk: 12 testers must stay opted in 14 continuous days (Help 14151465). Recruit 15, and land batch-outcome before the clock if the schedule allows.
- **Not verified here (suspended on test):** account type, Play Console state, trademark clearance, counsel opinion. These are B1/B5/B7 and cannot be closed from this tree.

## 3. Qiyas card
- **Al-Far':** close B6/B7 "before production" and let the closed test run first, because testers are not "the public".
- **Al-Asl:** the licence text this app already ships — `app/src/main/assets/licenses/LGPL-3.0.txt:81-107` ("Give prominent notice **with each copy**" and "Convey the Minimal Corresponding Source…", §4(a)/(d)(0)); `assets/licenses/LGPL-2.1.txt:228-238` ("accompany it with the complete corresponding machine-readable source code", §6). Verifiable in this tree.
- **Al-'Illa:** the duty attaches to each copy conveyed, not to a store listing; audience size and channel are incidental, and a tester's install is a copy.
- **Tanqih:** the words "public"/"closed", Play-vs-sideload, the number 12; the transferable part is *first conveyance starts the duty*.
- **Conditions:** the source must match the exact shipped revision; the offer must be usable (hosted source or a monitored mailbox) at that moment; otherwise the build itself is the breach.

## 4. Fatal flaw of the obvious plan — **Fasad al-I'tibar**
The premise "green gate + code-complete = ready to upload; B6/B7 are paperwork for production" is invalid twice over: the legal text must change *inside the build* (placeholders above), so today's green AAB cannot be the upload AAB; and the first tester install is conveyance, while the offer is text-only (`release-blockers.md:161-167`). The schedule, not just the timing, is wrong.
