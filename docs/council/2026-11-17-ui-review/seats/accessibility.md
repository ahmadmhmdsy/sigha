# Accessibility — Sigha UI review (static, read-only)

Scope: 20 files under `app/src/main/java/com/sigha/converter/ui`, `res/values*/strings*.xml`, and the custom Material 3 theme. Method: source inspection plus WCAG 2.x contrast computed from the actual hex values, *after* Material 3 tonal-elevation compositing (every card is `tonalElevation = 1.dp` on a `surfaceTint` derived from `primary`). No device, emulator, or TalkBack run — runtime behaviour is marked "inferred" where it depends on the platform.

## Verdict
The visual system is disciplined and the accessibility *plumbing* that is cheap is done correctly: every icon-only control has a localized `contentDescription`, every decorative icon is `null`, there is not one hard-coded UI string, Arabic string parity is 165/165 (contentDescriptions included), and green is never the only carrier of state. What is missing is everything above that floor: **there are zero explicit semantics modifiers in ~2,500 lines of Compose UI** — 0 `Modifier.semantics`, 0 `stateDescription`, 0 `heading()`, 0 `liveRegion`, and exactly one `Role.` (LicensesScreen.kt:317). Consequently the app is *navigable* by TalkBack but not *confirmable*: a screen-reader user can activate things but cannot hear what is selected, what value a slider holds, which file a percentage belongs to, or which format is the source and which the target. Add two concrete regressions that hit sighted users too — the light-theme contrast of the 12sp format badges and the "Done" pill (3.76–4.37:1, AA failure) and system-bar icon colour that follows the OS theme rather than the app's theme override — and this ships accessibility as "labels present, state absent".

## Strengths

1. **Icon-only controls are complete, localized, and honest.** Every `IconButton` in the app carries a `stringResource` description (back, view queue, clear selection, remove, open file, share, clear all) and every purely decorative icon — format tiles, empty-state art, arrows between formats, section bullets — is `contentDescription = null` with adjacent text carrying the meaning. Evidence: WorkbenchScreen.kt:156, 250, 350, 626; HistoryScreen.kt:79, 268, 274; QueueScreen.kt:294, 306; Common.kt:74; FormatUi.kt:106; AboutScreen.kt:82.
2. **Arabic is a first-class screen-reader language, not a fallback.** `values-ar/strings.xml` has exactly the 165 keys of `values/strings.xml` (programmatic diff: 0 missing, 0 extra, 0 placeholder mismatches), so every `contentDescription` and every status/failure headline a TalkBack user hears exists in Arabic; `strings_ui.xml` and `strings_legal.xml` are likewise 1:1 (values-ar/strings_ui.xml:4-42, values-ar/strings_legal.xml:4-27). ViewModel snackbar messages are localized via `context.getString` rather than baked-in English (WorkbenchViewModel.kt:97, 123, 227-229).
3. **Status is redundant across colour, text, and shape.** `StatusPill` prints the localized word ("Queued"/"Converting"/"Done"/"Failed"/"Cancelled") in the status colour on a 12% tint (Common.kt:169-198), the row icons differ by shape per status (WorkbenchScreen.kt:566-578, QueueScreen.kt:193-205), and failures add a text headline (QueueScreen.kt:236-241). A user who cannot see green still gets "Done". This is the model the rest of the app should follow.

## Persona red flags

**Amina, 47, Baghdad — runs her office from the phone, presbyopic, system font 1.3, keeps the phone in Light and reads Sigha in its default light theme.** What she hits first is the text that is both smallest and most repeated: the format badges on her result rows and the "Done" pill, set at 12sp in 3.76–4.0:1 contrast (FormatUi.kt:115-126, Common.kt:188-198). The privacy tagline she installed the app for is ellipsized in the top bar before she finishes reading it (Common.kt:57-65 + strings.xml:6). At night she flips Sigha to Dark while the phone stays in Light, and the status-bar icons switch to dark-on-dark over the app surface — she loses the clock and the battery (MainActivity.kt:37, inferred). The privacy policy she opens to check the app's claims is a wall of English (PrivacyScreen.kt:110-120).

**Kareem, 29, Baghdad — blind since birth, TalkBack at 2× rate, converts scanned PDFs for study.** He can pick files and tap Convert; what he cannot do is *hear his own decisions*: the quality slider says "0.85", not "image quality, 85 percent" (WorkbenchScreen.kt:446-451); the OCR language dialog costs two swipes per language and never says which one is current on the row (SettingsScreen.kt:272-296); a queued file announces "0 percent" while the pill says "Queued" (WorkbenchScreen.kt:591-597); his results row reads "JPG, PDF" with no direction (QueueScreen.kt:214-226); and the reason his batch failed is 12sp, two lines, ellipsized (HistoryScreen.kt:245-251). He cannot skim to Options or Progress because nothing is a heading (Common.kt:86-104). His verdict would be "it works, but it doesn't talk to me".

## Qiyas cards — top two recommendations

### Q1 — Stop using category hue as 12sp text colour; let the icon carry identity

**Al-Far' (proposal):** In light theme, `FormatBadge` becomes neutral text (`onSurfaceVariant`) on the accent's 12% tint (or on `surfaceVariant`), while the accent stays on the 44dp `FormatIcon` tile and on the badge's 1dp dot; `StatusPill` moves to the `primaryContainer`/`onPrimaryContainer` pair. All accents are re-verified at ≥4.5:1 as *text* only where they are text.

**Al-Asl (precedent):** Material 3's own colour-role contract and chip anatomy: `AssistChip`/`SuggestionChip` put `onSurface`/`onSurfaceVariant` text on a neutral container and reserve the accent for the leading icon and the *selected* state; Android's Files/Drive/Photos file-type rows do exactly this — coloured type glyph, neutral label. (Sigha already applies it correctly in `FormatIcon`, FormatUi.kt:96-110, and breaks it in `FormatBadge`, FormatUi.kt:115-126.) Source contrast rules: WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 (4.5:1 for normal text) and SC 1.4.11 (3:1 for meaningful graphics).

**Al-'Illa (causal invariant):** A hue can *identify* at icon scale where 3:1 suffices, but at text scale it must also *luminance-contrast* against its own background; a 12% tint of the same hue is a contrast-reducing transform on top of a tonal-elevation transform, so any accent bright enough to look "vivid" on white loses the 4.5:1 margin precisely where it is set smallest. Identity and readability are two different jobs; one colour cannot do both at 12sp.

**Conditions:** keep the 13 category hues and the dark-theme variants (dark currently passes 7–11:1 — do not touch it); keep `tonalElevation = 1.dp` cards and recompute against the *composited* card colour, not `surface`; the Arabic badge labels are Latin format codes so length is not a risk; verify with the actual `Format.label` values and at 200% font scale (badge padding must not clip); high-contrast/colour-correction modes must still distinguish categories by icon shape.

### Q2 — Make each choice one node: `selectableGroup` + `selectable(role = RadioButton)`, glyph is not a control

**Al-Far' (proposal):** The OCR-language dialog and the two output-mode rows become a `Column(Modifier.selectableGroup())` of rows with `Modifier.selectable(selected, onClick, role = Role.RadioButton)`; the `RadioButton` receives `onClick = null` and only draws.

**Al-Asl (precedent):** Android Settings' "Dark theme" radio rows and the M3 accessibility guidance for `Modifier.selectableGroup()`: the platform's own radio list is one focus stop per option, announcing "…, selected, radio button", and the visible circle is drawn by the row. Material 3 `ListItem` + `RadioButton` sample does the same.

**Al-'Illa (causal invariant):** A mutually exclusive choice has exactly one selectable node per option, and the selected state lives on that node; duplicating the node duplicates and can *desynchronise* state (two taps, two announcements, two places to be wrong). The glyph is a rendering, not a control.

**Conditions:** RTL mirroring is automatic for `RadioButton` but the row's leading/trailing order must be checked in Arabic; the dialog must return focus to the OCR row that opened it; `selectableGroup()` must wrap only the option set, not the dialog title/confirm; the same pattern should then be applied to the theme `FilterChip` group for consistency (chips already expose selection, so this is a rename-level change).

## Fatal flaw

**The app has an accessibility vocabulary of labels only — no state.** Sigha speaks to TalkBack in bare nouns: 0 explicit semantics modifiers in 20 UI files, one role in the entire product, no headings, no `stateDescription`, no live region, a slider with no name, radio choices delivered as two separate controls, progress that never says which file, and a source→target line that reads as two unrelated codes. The consequence is not that the core flow is unreachable — a determined screen-reader user can still pick a file, pick a chip labelled "PDF" and press "Convert" — it is that **nothing that the visual design encodes by position, tint, glyph or 12% alpha is confirmed back to them**, so every completion of the flow is an act of trust rather than verification. For an Arabic-first, offline, no-account converter whose whole pitch is "you stay in control", that is the mismatch that matters most: control without feedback. The fix is also unusually cheap — one semantics pass over shared components (`SectionHeader`, `StatusPill`, `FormatBadge`, the progress row, the radio rows) covers every screen.

## Questions the design team should answer

1. **Which is the source language of the product?** The UI is Arabic; the privacy policy and licence texts are English-only. If Arabic is the promise, which document is authoritative, and who writes the Arabic privacy policy?
2. **What minimum Android font scale must the app survive — 1.3, 1.5, or 2.0?** The two-line 64dp app bar only works at ~1.3. Decide, then either move the tagline or accept a taller bar.
3. **Is format category colour meant to identify or decorate?** Seven of thirteen light-theme accents cannot legally be 12sp text. Was the badge tint a deliberate brand decision, and is a neutral-label + coloured-glyph badge acceptable?
4. **Why does "Done" use emerald-on-emerald at 3.99:1 when the container pair (#002019 on #B8EBDD) is 13:1?** Is the status pill's colour meant to be read as text or as a tinted affordance?
5. **When the user's in-app theme disagrees with the OS, which wins for the status bar?** Today the OS wins, and the app's own theme selector breaks its chrome.
6. **Should selection rows be single-stop M3 radio rows?** Today's double-stop rows look accidental (no `selectableGroup` anywhere) — confirm the intended pattern before it is copied to theme chips and future settings.
7. **Should TalkBack users be able to jump between sections?** If yes, `SectionHeader` becoming a heading is a one-line change that retrofits every screen; if no, say why.
